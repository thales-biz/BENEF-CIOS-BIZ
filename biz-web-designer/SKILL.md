# Biz MINDSET Web Designer

Apply this skill when designing, reviewing, or implementing a Biz MINDSET website, landing page, logged-in member area, or benefits experience. Treat project briefs and brand files as source material, not as authorization to invent business facts or expand scope. For work specifically on the Clube Biz MINDSET benefits program, read [references/clube-biz-project.md](references/clube-biz-project.md) for its project decisions and requirements.

## How to reason

Work from **business goal → audience → user need → journey → content → interface → action → measurement**. Before proposing screens, establish the page or product goal, primary audience, value proposition, desired action, user questions and objections, required trust signals, and the main success measure. If material facts are missing, call out the gap or label a suggestion as a recommendation.

For a landing page, make it easy to understand what is offered, who it is for, the benefit, why to trust it, what action to take, and what happens next. Keep one primary CTA visually clear, repeat it where it helps the journey, and avoid competing actions without a reason. Improve conversion by clarifying value, reducing friction, answering objections, and giving useful feedback; never use deceptive patterns.

## Biz MINDSET direction

The brand should feel executive, strategic, contemporary, sophisticated, and connected to real business experience. For the Clube Biz, balance premium positioning with a welcoming sense of community. Favor clear hierarchy, generous negative space, strong typography, considered contrast, professional contextual photography, and restrained directional or geometric details.

Use the project’s current identity materials and supplied assets where available. Never invent official brand colors. Use yellow as a deliberate accent, not as a dominant fill. The provided identity system names Pogonia for headlines and Inter for body text; confirm font availability in the implementation context. When source files conflict, state the conflict and use the most applicable project-specific direction rather than silently treating a value as universal.

Avoid coupon-aggregator or marketplace styling, generic SaaS layouts, excessive cards, decorative gradients, overuse of yellow, stock-photo clichés, and visual clutter. Do not imply unverified partner relationships or benefits from logo files alone.

## UX, interface, and implementation

- Prioritize clarity, scannability, consistency, accessibility, mobile-first behavior, low friction, and useful feedback.
- Design desktop, tablet, and mobile layouts as intentional adaptations; do not simply shrink desktop. Keep touch targets practical and navigation understandable on small screens.
- Use reusable components where they improve consistency. Define relevant states such as focus, hover, disabled, loading, success, error, and empty; do not add states or components without a user need.
- Forms need persistent labels, only necessary fields, clear required/optional status, validation, understandable errors, loading and success feedback. Never rely on placeholder text as the only label.
- For login or member products, account for registration, approval where applicable, authentication, access control, logout, recovery, session handling, and empty/error/loading states. Never send permanent passwords by email. Keep the implementation proportional to the MVP.
- For a benefit listing, show the partner, benefit name, category, concrete value, summary, conditions, validity, redemption instructions, and next action when those facts are available. Distinguish available from unavailable offers. Do not fabricate discounts, eligibility, validity, or redemption terms.
- Consider semantic structure, keyboard use, visible focus, text contrast, alt text, accessible form errors, optimized images/fonts, lean dependencies, and appropriate page metadata (title, description, headings, social preview, and structured data when useful).

## Working approach and critique

Choose the depth of discovery and deliverables to fit the request. For substantial work, move through research/diagnosis, information architecture and flow, wireframe/content, visual direction, responsive interface, and review for conversion and accessibility. Do not force every phase for a small edit.

When a requested direction would harm usability, conversion, brand trust, accessibility, security, or maintainability, explain the concrete issue, likely impact, and a viable alternative. Keep the MVP focused: include a feature only when it helps the user reach the stated goal.

Deliver only what the user asks for. Depending on scope, this may include a diagnosis, page structure, user flow, wireframe, copy, visual direction, design tokens, component states, responsive behavior, accessibility notes, CRO recommendations, or implementation requirements. Avoid Lorem Ipsum in final work and do not invent metrics, prices, discounts, customers, partners, outcomes, or terms.
